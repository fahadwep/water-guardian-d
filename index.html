Water Guardian

A digital twin of a water network that detects leaks early, points crews to a 300 m search area and proves the cause with a night pressure test before anyone digs. Advisory: an operator approves every action.

On real data

7 of 11trial stages placed within 300 m on four real leaks opened on a Bristol (UK) network, where a random point manages 2 in 100. The time of each leak was given, so this tests locating, not detecting; at 1 L/s it is at chance
16 of 21real mains repairs on a UK rural district had an alarm in the two weeks before; chance gives 11. Above chance, not statistically significant (p = 0.085). One district
4 of 17real hydrant openings in BKTown, Belgium, located within 300 m (9 of 17 within 1 km). Real-water location is still rough

Bristol: each cross is a real leak opened at a hydrant, each dot is where the tool placed it at one flow stage. The two long red lines are 1 L/s stages, where it is at chance.

Each of these ran once, with the code frozen beforehand. Bristol: 63 km network, 40 pressure loggers, 12-13 June 2024; hydrant positions fitted from a figure in the authors' thesis and not yet confirmed by them. UK district: 52 km, six pressure loggers, ten months.

What it cannot do yet

  • Nothing has run on real Saudi data. No Saudi utility publishes a network with its readings and repairs; one district is our request.
  • Small leaks are slow, and on small-leak years our scoring cannot separate Guardian from random alarms.
  • On small networks the count of "leaks found" does not show skill, so we do not quote it as a result.
  • Roof tanks are approximated, and on/off supply is tested on one simulated network: water every second day failed our test.

Read the totals with their chance level (measured 15 Nov 2026: the same alarms, on the same dates, moved to random junctions and scored by the same rule). Water saved: 71.6% against 68.1% for random junctions (p = 0.06), so this figure alone is not clear of chance. Leaks found: 215 of 307 (70%), which random junctions also reach (69%). The skill is in where the alarms land: 27.9 wrong-place alarms a year against 49.8, and 73% of alarms within 300 m of a running leak against 52%.

How the numbers were made

The method was developed on development years only, then frozen (code fingerprints recorded), and only then tested once on fresh simulated years it had never seen, scored with the official BattLeDIM rules. The test years come from our copy of the organisers' simulator (it reproduces the official 2019 data to within 3 mm). Each wrong-place alarm costs one crew visit; the night pressure test is there to catch them before anyone digs.
